Reading the pronunciation

Written for English speakers. CAPITALS show which part of the word to stress.

ah ay ee oh ooLong vowels: ah as in "father", ay as in "day", ee as in "see", oh as in "go", oo as in "food". tahk = Tag.
a e i o uShort vowels, quick and clipped: a as in "cat" (but further back), e as in "bed", i as in "sit", o as in "hot", u as in "put".
uh / erA weak, unstressed ending: BI-tuh = bitte, BE-ser = besser (don't say the r).
ewü — say "ee" with your lips pushed into a tight circle. MEW-duh = müde.
urö — like "fur" without the r, lips rounded. shurn = schön.
eye / oy / owei, eu/äu and au: mine = mein, HOY-tuh = heute, hows = Haus.
shThe soft ch in ich, nicht, Milch and words ending in -ig. It's really a hiss between "sh" and the h in "huge". ish = ich.
khThe throaty ch after a, o, u and au, like the ch in Scottish "loch". bookh = Buch, owkh = auch.
tsGerman z (and tz) — like the end of "cats". tsoo = zu.
v / f / zw sounds like v (vas = was), v usually like f (feel = viel), and s before a vowel like z (ZAH-gen = sagen).
shp / shtsp and st at the start of a word: SHPEE-len = spielen.
