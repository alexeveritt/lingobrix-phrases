Reading the pronunciation

Written for English speakers. French gives every syllable about the same weight, with a slight lift at the end of a phrase, so nothing is in capitals. Words that run together are joined with hyphens: voo-za-vay = vous avez.

a / ay / eh / uha as in "cat", ay as in "day" (é, -er, -ez), eh as in "bed" (è, ê, ai), uh the weak e in le, de, je.
ee / oh / o / ooee as in "see", oh as in "go" (au, eau), o as in "hot", oo as in "food" (ou).
ewFrench u — say "ee" with your lips pushed into a tight circle. tew = tu.
ureu before a sounded consonant, like "fur" without the r. sur = sœur.
ohn / ahn / an / uhnNasal vowels (on, an/en, in/ain, un): don't say the n — let the sound come through your nose. bohn = bon, ma-mahn = maman, pan = pain.
wa / weeoi and ui: mwa = moi, nwee = nuit.
zhj, and g before e/i — like the s in "measure". zhuh = je.
sh / nych is "sh" (sha = chat); gn is like "canyon" (mohn-tany = montagne).
rFrench r is made at the back of the throat, a bit like a soft gargle.
(silent)h is never said, and most final consonants and final e are silent: puh-tee = petit, tabl = table.
