Reading the pronunciation

Written for English speakers. CAPITALS show which part of the word to stress.

ah eh ee oh ooSpanish vowels are short and clean: ah as in "father", eh as in "bed", ee as in "see", oh as in "hot", oo as in "food".
thSpain's c (before e/i) and z — like "think". THEEN-koh = cinco.
hSpanish j, and g before e/i — like an English h but stronger and breathier, from the back of the throat. DEH-hah = deja, KEH-hehs = quejes.
bb and v sound the same, a soft b. BAH-leh = vale.
yll and y — like "yes". YAH-behs = llaves.
nyñ — like "canyon". mah-NYAH-nah = mañana.
rrA rolled r (rr, or r at the start of a word). A single r is a quick tap, like the tt in American "better".
(silent)A written Spanish h is never sounded: OH-rah = hora.
